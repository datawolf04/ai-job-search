## STAR Format

Structure answers as: **Situation** (context), **Task** (your responsibility), **Action** (what you did), **Result** (outcome).

Keep answers to 1-2 minutes. Be specific. End with what you learned or would do differently.

## Ready-Made STAR Examples

### 1. NSF-IUSE Grant Secured ($900K) (Leadership, Research Development)
**S:** The ECU Physics Department needed funding to transform introductory physics labs and support undergraduate research across multiple STEM disciplines.
**T:** As PI, I needed to develop a compelling research proposal, coordinate with co-PIs across departments, and secure federal funding from NSF-IUSE.
**A:** Developed the research design and proposal, coordinated with team members to align objectives, submitted to NSF-IUSE, and managed the $900K award through implementation.
**R:** Secured $900K in federal funding; project supported multiple graduate students and produced tools used across departments.
**Use for:** "Tell me about a time you led a project", "Describe your experience with grant writing/funding"

### 2. ECU Learning Assistant Program (Program Leadership, Cross-Functional Collaboration)
**S:** The ECU Learning Assistant Program needed sustainable funding and a structured implementation plan to scale across STEM departments.
**T:** As Program Director, I needed to develop the program, secure permanent funding, and evaluate its impact across multiple departments.
**A:** Led program planning, implementation, and evaluation; secured permanent funding through the Dean's office; coordinated with faculty across departments to adopt the model.
**R:** Over $500K in funding secured during leadership term; program became permanently funded and adopted across multiple STEM departments.
**Use for:** "Describe a time you managed a program or initiative", "How do you work with cross-functional teams?"

### 3. Data Management Steering Committee (Stakeholder Communication, Translation)
**S:** ECU needed a faculty representative to translate technical data management needs into policy on the university's Data Management Steering Committee.
**T:** Serve as the bridge between technical stakeholders and policy makers, translating complex needs into actionable recommendations.
**A:** Attended committee meetings, gathered requirements from faculty and staff, translated technical needs into policy language, and published 10 technical reports to support the committee's work.
**R:** Successfully bridged technical and non-technical stakeholders; contributed to data governance policies; published 10 technical reports.
**Use for:** "How do you communicate technical concepts to non-technical audiences?", "Tell me about a time you had to translate between technical and business teams"

### 4. PERC Proceedings Editorial Leadership (Team Leadership, Process Improvement)
**S:** The Physics Education Research Conference Proceedings needed to convert from single-blind to double-blind peer review during the COVID pandemic.
**T:** As Editor in Chief, I needed to lead the editorial team through this transition while maintaining quality and meeting conference deadlines.
**A:** Created a timeline for the conversion, introduced it to the community, led the editorial team through the process, and coordinated with conference planning groups.
**R:** Successfully converted to double-blind review; maintained editorial standards; created a fully virtual conference during COVID.
**Use for:** "Describe a time you led a team through change", "How do you handle process improvement?"

## Questions You Should Ask Interviewers

### About the Role
- "What does a typical week look like in this role?"
- "What would success look like in the first 6 months?"
- "What's the biggest challenge the team is facing right now?"

### About the Team
- "How big is the team, and how do you divide work?"
- "What does the development/project lifecycle look like, from idea to production?"
- "How do you onboard new team members?"

### About Tech & Growth
- "What's your current tech stack for [relevant area]?"
- "Is there room to grow into more architectural or strategic decisions?"
- "How does the team stay current with new tools and methods?"

### About Culture (use these to prevent disappointment)
- "How would you describe the team culture?"
- "What does professional development look like here?"
- "Is there flexibility for remote/hybrid work?"
- "What's the balance between development/new projects and maintenance work?"
- "How would you describe the leadership style in this team?"
- "What do people who thrive here have in common?"

## Phone/Video Interview Tips
- Have STAR examples written out (use this file)
- Keep a glass of water nearby
- Smile when speaking (it changes your tone)
- Ask for clarification if a question is vague
- It's OK to take 5 seconds to think before answering
- End with: "Is there anything else you'd like to know about my background?"

## After the Application (Best Practice)

### Follow-Up Etiquette
- **Don't call to "stand out"** or to learn more about the role post-submission - this risks a negative impression
- If the employer specified a timeline, respect it and wait
- If no timeline was given and significant time has passed (2+ weeks), a brief call to ask about status is acceptable
- If you have genuinely new, relevant information to share, a short follow-up is fine

### Thank-You Notes
- When you receive any update (interview invitation, rejection, or status update), send a brief thank-you message
- Express appreciation for their time and the process
- Keep it short (2-3 sentences)

## Roleplay Guidelines
When the user asks for interview practice:
1. Ask which role/company to simulate
2. Start with easy warm-up questions ("Tell me about yourself")
3. Progress to role-specific technical questions
4. Include 1-2 behavioral questions using the competencies from the job posting
5. End with a tough question or curveball
6. After each answer, give brief feedback: what worked, what to sharpen
7. Suggest which STAR example would work best for each question
